\begin{abstract}
Learning-based circuit modeling is central to many AI-for-EDA (AI4EDA) tasks, yet developing accurate predictors requires substantial task-specific expertise and repeated cycles of model design, training, and tuning. Effective models must align circuit representations, neural computation, and learning objectives with the underlying EDA task, making this process difficult to automate through architecture or hyperparameter search alone. We present CircuitModelPilot, an agentic framework that automates this modeling workflow across AI4EDA tasks. Given a task specification and an initial model, LLM agents formulate modeling hypotheses grounded in EDA semantics, implement candidate designs, conduct training and evaluation, and refine subsequent proposals using experimental feedback. The framework organizes exploration as a model-design search tree: each node records a candidate model and its experimental evidence, while each edge represents a hypothesis-driven modification to its parent. Search proceeds by selecting and expanding promising candidates, revisiting earlier alternatives, and pruning unproductive directions. This process explores circuit representations, neural architectures and propagation mechanisms, and learning objectives, with a separate inner search for training hyperparameters. Structured experiment records, scoped code changes, and a consistent evaluation protocol support reproducible comparisons and preserve knowledge from both successful and unsuccessful trials. We establish an evaluation across HLS resource prediction, netlist-level timing prediction, and RTL-to-netlist timing prediction to assess predictive performance relative to human-designed models and automated-search baselines, as well as search efficiency and execution reliability. CircuitModelPilot aims to reduce manual modeling effort while systematically pursuing task-specific models that match or exceed human-designed baselines.
\end{abstract}
